Built for people who'd rather delegate than read

"Lazy" is the honest word for it. The real problem is attention rationing — reading things you didn't choose to read, typing the same details into the seventh form this month.

The desk-slacker

Wants the gist, not the document

A browser full of dashboards, tickets and half-read documents. Wants the status update written for them so they can go back to what they actually care about.

The student

Wants answers grounded in the page

Twelve tabs of readings due tomorrow. Wants questions answered against the page in front of them — not a general chatbot that hasn't read it.

The form-hater

Hates it enough to abandon the flow

The same name, address, phone, employer — over and over, on every signup, application, and booking on the internet.

One button. Three verbs.

Click Summon, or press ⌘J, and the panel opens already knowing the page. Everything after that is one of three things.

Ask

Answers, with receipts

Grounded in the page you're on — or every open tab, if you ask across them. Every answer cites the part of the page it came from; click the citation and it scrolls right to it.

Fill

Forms, done for you

Scans the page, works out what each field wants, and fills it from your Profile Card. Guesses get flagged for a second look. Nothing is ever submitted.

Do

The rest of the busywork

Rewrite this shorter. Draft a reply. Translate this page. Summarize this thread. One click inserts the result straight into the field you were looking at.

What's actually in the box

Ten features, six worth naming up front — the rest is in the fine print.

F1

Summon panel

Opens as a resizable side panel that reflows the page instead of covering it — the page stays visible and clickable the whole time.

F2

Page Q&A with citations

The single most-used action. Citations are what stop it from being a confident liar.

F3

Auto-fill from your Profile Card

Detects fields, fills from stored facts, flags its own guesses. Never touches a password or payment field.

F6

Draft & Insert

Turns "help me write this" into the reply actually being there, in the field you had focused.

F7

Ask across tabs

Scoped to every open tab in the window — for the "which of these did I want" problem.

F9

Summary on demand

A keyboard shortcut that summarizes the page into the panel with no typing at all.
